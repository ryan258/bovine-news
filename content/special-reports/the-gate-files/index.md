## Chapter One — A Door, Deliberately

Every investigation begins with something small. Ours began with hardware.

Sometime between 11 p.m. on August 12 and dawn on August 13, someone opened the main gate of the north pasture. They did not break it. They did not climb it. By every measurement this desk is capable of, they operated it — smoothly, quietly, and with what forensics has repeatedly described as "suspiciously good manners."

The latch was found latched back against the fence, the way a careful person leaves a gate when they intend to return, or the way a very confident one leaves it when they don't. The hinges had been oiled recently enough that investigators initially assumed maintenance. No work order exists. The oil, laboratory analysis confirmed, was the good oil. The expensive one. The bottle that was supposed to be reserved for the tractor.

"We are treating this as a serious breach of pasture security," a county spokesperson told reporters, declining to speculate on motive while standing directly beside a wheelbarrow of empty ranch-dressing packets that nobody present would acknowledge.

Thirty-seven packets, by our count. We have counted twice.

## Chapter Two — Eighteen Months of Rhyming

This is not the first time the gate has been open. It is only the first time anyone has admitted what that might mean.

In February 2025, the same gate was found open under nearly identical conditions, and the county closed the file in under an hour by blaming the wind. It was a tidy solution. It was also, we now know, generous. The wind has never once been polite, and politeness is the signature of this case — the neat latch, the oiled hinges, the note.

The note deserves its own paragraph. Wedged beneath the latch sometime before dawn, typed on paper stock matching the feed shed ledger, punctuated flawlessly, it reads, in its entirety:

> "Thank you for the opportunity."

Investigators have spent weeks asking what opportunity, exactly, was being thanked. The salad bar forty yards beyond the gate opened for business at 6 a.m. sharp. Its register tape shows a transaction at 3:11 a.m. Total: $0.00. Payment method: tab. The salad bar was closed at the time. It has no tab system.

When you lay the eighteen months out in sequence — and we have laid them out below — the pattern stops looking like coincidence and starts looking like patience. Someone has been preparing for this door the way other people prepare for holidays.

## Chapter Three — The Evidence Speaks, Politely

The physical evidence in this case is unusual in that there is plenty of it and none of it accuses anybody. Every exhibit has been logged, photographed, and described by technicians using words like "considered" and "composed." Security footage from the feed shed captured eleven continuous minutes of a single cow staring directly into the lens. Audio analysis detected no speech, no alarm, and no wind — only chewing, which analysts characterized as "unhurried."

A hoofprint cast poured by this desk from a coffee can and professional desperation places a 1,400-pound cloven visitor at the scene, taking their time. Stride analysis suggests someone who knew precisely where they were going and saw no reason to rush either direction.

## Chapter Four — The Lineup

Four persons of interest. No charges. The desk presents them below in the interest of the public's right to squint.

What unites the suspects is not opportunity, though all four had it, nor motive, though the salad bar provides one to everyone within walking distance. What unites them is that each, when asked where they were on the night of August 12, gave an answer this desk found interesting. One said "here" five times unprompted. One sent a written statement reading, in full, "Moo." One is the wind. The fourth exists only as a composite sketch drawn from witness descriptions, every one of which was "a cow."

If you recognize yourself or a loved one in the lineup below, the desk reminds you that coming forward voluntarily looks better at sentencing, whatever sentencing turns out to be around here.
