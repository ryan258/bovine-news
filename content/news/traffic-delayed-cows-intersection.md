Morning commuters faced nearly an hour of delays after a small herd wandered into the intersection of Main Street and Fifth Avenue during peak traffic and, rather than crossing, stopped to have what several witnesses independently described as "a meeting," according to county dispatch records.

## What Happened

Dispatch received the first call shortly after 7 a.m., reporting six cows arranged in a loose circle in the middle of the intersection, apparently deep in conversation, occasionally nodding. Officers arrived within minutes to redirect traffic while animal control attempted to determine what, exactly, was being discussed, a question animal control has not yet answered and, off the record, does not expect to.

- No injuries were reported, though one commuter reports honking politely and receiving what she swears was a dirty look.
- The intersection reopened to normal traffic by 8:15 a.m., shortly after the herd appeared to reach a consensus and departed single file, in a specific and deliberate order.
- Officials believe a damaged section of fencing along the adjacent property allowed the herd to reach the road, though this does not explain why they chose that particular intersection, at that particular hour, to hold that particular meeting.

"We ask drivers to remain patient in these situations — safety comes first, for the herd and for commuters, and also I would just like to know what they were talking about," said a county dispatch spokesperson, going slightly off script.

## Fence Repairs Underway

The property owner has been notified and says repairs to the damaged fencing will be completed by the end of the week, adding that this is the third such "gathering" reported near that stretch of fence this year and that he has, personally, stopped asking questions.
