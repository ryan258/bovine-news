A months-long Bovine News investigation has uncovered an unpermitted salt lick operating in plain sight behind a feed supply warehouse on County Road 9, raising urgent questions about oversight of local mineral supplement distribution and, according to several witnesses, "the single most transcendent thirty seconds of my adult life."

## How the Investigation Started

The story began with a single anonymous tip submitted through our [contact page]({{< relref "contact" >}}) in the spring, describing "unusual gathering patterns" near the warehouse's rear lot — a phrase that turned out to mean a line of forty cows standing in respectful, almost reverent silence, waiting their turn.

Records obtained through a public-records request show the site was never issued the required supplement distribution permit, a fact that has done nothing to slow demand. One regular described the lick's flavor profile as "hay-forward, with a smoky finish," which investigators note is not a phrase anyone should be able to say about a mineral block.

> "The herd deserves to know," said investigative reporter Bessie Barnes, who has led the inquiry since March and has, for the record, visited the lick eleven times purely for research purposes.

## What Records Show

- The warehouse's operating permit does not list mineral supplement distribution, or, as the warehouse's own internal memo referred to it, "the side thing."
- Neighboring landowners reported increased foot traffic beginning in late winter, alongside what one described as "a suspicious number of cows walking home looking very pleased with themselves."
- County officials confirmed no inspection had been conducted at the site in over two years, a gap the warehouse's owner attributes to "everyone being too polite to bring it up."
- A hand-lettered sign at the site reads "SUGGESTED DONATION: ONE (1) COMPLIMENT," which investigators are treating as a possible currency violation.

## Response and Next Steps

The warehouse's ownership group has not responded to a request for comment, though a source close to the operation says they are "not worried, because it's really good salt." County officials say they plan to schedule an inspection in the coming weeks, assuming the inspector, like everyone else who has gone near the site, does not simply stay.
