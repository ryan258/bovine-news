The city council voted 5-2 late Tuesday to approve the long-debated Creekside Park expansion plan, ending nearly a year of public hearings, seven redesigns, and a forty-minute stretch of testimony devoted entirely to whether a single proposed bench faced "too much sun" or "the correct, character-building amount of sun."

## The Vote

Council members cited overwhelming public turnout in favor of the expansion, which will add a new walking trail, a covered pavilion, and an expanded grazing lawn along the creek's western bank. The vote followed a hearing so long that two council members were observed chewing cud in what minutes later described as "an unofficial and unauthorized recess."

"This has been a long process, and I think the final plan reflects what residents actually asked for, once you subtract the bench thing," said council chair Holstein Marsh, visibly relieved, following the vote.

## What Changed

The approved plan differs from the original proposal in several ways:

- The parking area was reduced by a third after resident objections, chief among them a laminated petition titled "Cars Are Not Why We Come Here."
- A dedicated calf play area was added following a petition with over 400 signatures, several of which were, upon inspection, hoofprints in wet cement.
- Construction will now occur in two phases instead of one, to reduce disruption during festival season and, per the final motion, "to give the bench situation room to breathe."
- The bench itself was ultimately relocated four feet to the left, a compromise both sides described, separately, as "a total loss."

## Timeline

Construction is expected to begin this fall, with the first phase targeted for completion before next summer's Creekside Festival. Council members have asked that any further bench-related concerns be submitted in writing, and, ideally, never.
