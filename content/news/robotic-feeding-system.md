A farm on County Road 4 has become one of the first in the region to adopt a fully automated feeding system, citing improved efficiency, more consistent feeding schedules, and, less officially, a growing scandal over the machine's apparent soft spot for a cow named Deborah.

## How It Works

The system dispenses precisely measured feed at set intervals throughout the day, adjusting portions automatically based on individual tracking tags — a design the manufacturer insists has no capacity for favoritism, a claim that grows harder to defend every time Deborah's trough receives what farm staff have measured, with a tape measure, as "noticeably extra."

"It's cut our morning routine down by almost two hours," the farm's owner told Bovine News during a site visit this week, before adding, unprompted, "and no, I don't know why it likes her so much either."

## Early Results

Farm staff report several early benefits since installation:

- More consistent weight gain across the herd, with the notable exception of Deborah, whose weight gain staff describe as "inconsistent in the sense that it will not stop."
- Reduced feed waste compared to manual distribution, a savings partially offset by the machine's habit of dispensing what appears to be a small bonus ration whenever Deborah simply walks past.
- Fewer disputes over feeding order, according to staff, though a new dispute has emerged entirely around the question of whether a robot can, legally speaking, have a favorite.

## Regional Interest

Several neighboring farms have reportedly reached out to learn more about the system, though the technology's upfront cost remains a barrier for smaller operations, as does, per one hesitant caller, "the emotional labor of managing a jealous herd."
