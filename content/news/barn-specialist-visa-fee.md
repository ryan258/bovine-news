Local farmers reacted with alarm Tuesday to a newly enacted county fee of $103,000 per visa for hiring out-of-barn specialists, a category that covers foreign-trained border collies, migratory barn owls, and, under a drafting error nobody has corrected because everyone wants to see how it plays out, one particular goat.

## What the Fee Covers

The measure, intended to protect domestic barn labor, applies to any specialist whose expertise originates beyond the property's fence line. Farm operators say the practical effect is immediate: two sheepdog placements from out of county are on hold, a beloved owl relocation has been downgraded to "occasional visits, unpaid, please just visit," and three farms have reopened searches for locally sourced talent that the postings now describe, optimistically, as "any dog."

"We did the math," said Marlene Okafor, who operates a dairy operation east of town. "One hundred three thousand dollars per collie. I can buy a lot of fence. I can buy, and I want to be clear about this, a different farm."

## The Cats

Domestic barn cats are unaffected by the fee, having naturalized generations ago through channels the county describes as "grandfathered" and the cats describe, reportedly, as "settled law." Several farms noted this makes the cats the most cost-effective staff on the payroll, a fact the cats have allegedly internalized.

"One hundred three thousand dollars," said Okafor. "The cats heard the number. I watched them hear it. They have never once respected me and this has widened the gap."

## What Happens Next

The county board has scheduled a review hearing, though members privately concede the fee was designed to be negotiated rather than paid, calling it "a number that starts conversations." Two conversations have already started, both initiated by goats, whose eligibility under the drafting error remains the county's fastest-growing legal question.

Farms needing herding expertise this season are advised to practice whistling, lower their expectations, or, per one agricultural extension agent, "get really good at opening and closing gates yourself, which, given the year we've had, nobody here can be trusted to do."
