The county elections office announced emergency proceedings this morning to limit mail-in balloting, one day after all 412 registered voters in the north pasture submitted their pasture council ballots in a single envelope, postmarked 3 a.m., with return address "the field next to the other field."

## What Happened

Under the herd's traditional voting custom, each member fills out a ballot, chews it into a distinctive personal fold, and gives it to whichever cow is walking toward the mailbox anyway. This year, that cow was the same cow who opened the gate, according to nobody official, and the resulting bloc vote arrived so unified that election staff assumed it was a single extremely ambitious voter.

"The rule has always been one ballot per voter," said County Clerk Dale Hutchins, holding the envelope with both hands. "It does not say anything about one envelope per electorate. That is a gap we did not know we had, and I want it noted that we were happier before we knew."

## The Ruling

A tie among sitting council members over whether to count the votes was broken when the herd's eldest member arrived at the courthouse, regarded the envelope for eleven seconds, and slow-nodded, a gesture the county attorney has provisionally classified as binding precedent.

The ballots themselves are being reviewed individually. Each one is legible. Each one is folded the same way. Elections officials describe this as "either fraud or unity, and honestly at this point it comes down to paperwork."

> "Every vote should count," said one herd member, through a translator who declined to be named because she is also the mail carrier. "Just maybe not all at once, and not all weighing nine pounds."

## What Happens Next

Proposed reforms include requiring one envelope per voter, banning coalition folding, and installing a second mailbox, which the budget committee estimates will take six hours of debate, minimum.

The pasture council election remains scheduled for September. The herd has indicated it will vote early, together, and again in one envelope, which the clerk describes as "technically compliant with whatever rules exist at the moment of mailing."

That's the story. The herd could not be reached for separate comment, because separate is exactly what this story is about.
