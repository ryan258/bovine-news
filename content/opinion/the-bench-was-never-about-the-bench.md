I sat through all six hours of Tuesday's city council meeting, and I want to be very clear about something before the revisionism starts: the vote — after six hours, three recesses, one parliamentary inquiry, and a formal reading of the word "bench" into the record forty-one times — was never about the bench.

The Creekside Park expansion plan passed with broad support. Everyone in that chamber wanted the park. The park was, and I am quoting Councilwoman Ferreira directly, "the easy part." What this community learned on Tuesday is that there is no easy part.

## Follow the seating

The dispute, as the official minutes will tell it, concerned the placement of a single memorial bench near the creek overlook, with a small plaque, the wording of which consumed roughly ninety minutes of deliberation on its own. The minutes will not tell you why. The minutes never do. That is what an opinion column is for.

Here is what I observed from the press table. Every council member who spoke against the bench praised the bench. They called it lovely. Appropriate. A fitting tribute. One member became visibly emotional describing its craftsmanship. None of them voted for it until the final roll call, at which point it passed unanimously, and everyone applauded, including the people who had spent four hours explaining why it could not go anywhere near the overlook.

> You can learn everything about a town from what its council argues about when they agree on the outcome. The bench was just where everybody decided to have the argument they'd been storing up all summer.

Because here is what the bench was actually about: the overlook. And the overlook was actually about the view of the north pasture. And the north pasture was actually about the gate, which — as our readers know — was found standing open last week under circumstances the county grazing authority has described, twice daily, as "an active and ongoing situation."

Nobody on that council said the word gate. Not once. I checked my notes. But every speech about sight lines, every question about drainage, every concern about "what people might see from the new path" had ears pointing firmly west.

## What happens now

The park will be beautiful. The bench will be sturdy, well-situated, and engraved, per the final language, with text "agreed in committee." The expansion breaks ground next spring, and this community will be better for it.

And when the investigation across the fence line finally concludes — when we learn who oiled those hinges and why the salad bar was involved — I would ask you to remember where your council members stood on the bench, because I promise you they were not standing where they said.

Let's get to the bottom of this. We always do.
