At 7:40 Tuesday morning I got the dispatch every field reporter trains for and almost none of us ever get: cows, intersection, forming up, bring the long lens. Traffic on Main Street was already backing up in both directions when I arrived, which is standard. What was not standard was the agenda.

I have covered cow gatherings for my entire career. I want to be respectful here, because several of the participants are sources, one is my mail carrier's sister, and all of them outweigh me. But professionally speaking, what happened on Main Street between 8:02 and 9:34 a.m. was the most disciplined, most deliberate, and least informative event I have ever attended, including two county budget hearings.

## The minutes, as far as anyone can reconstruct them

- 8:02 — The herd arrives, not as a group but in shifts of six to eight, which traffic analysts confirm is the most disruptive possible configuration. Nobody honks. Everyone knows better.
- 8:15 — A large cow takes position at the centerline. I recognize her; she has been quoted in this paper before and has never once been wrong. She says nothing. This appears to be the chair.
- 8:31 — A motion is proposed, in the sense that everyone simultaneously turns to look at the pharmacy sign. It carries unanimously. There are no nays. There are no words.
- 9:04 — A brief recess. Several members graze the courthouse lawn median, which I believe is municipal property, and which no code enforcement officer in this county will approach, correctly.
- 9:30 — The chair scans the crowd — I want to be clear that at this point she looked directly into my camera for eleven seconds, which our photo desk has confirmed is a message, though not what it means — and gives a single slow nod.
- 9:34 — Adjournment. The herd exits eastbound in perfect order. Main Street reopens. No statement is issued. No follow-up questions are taken, or technically asked, or answerable in any language I have access to.

> Real journalism is showing up with a microphone to an event that has no sound. I stood in that intersection for ninety-two minutes and the only quote I came home with was from a crossing guard, who said, "They do this."

## What we know

We know the meeting was scheduled, because shifts arrived too cleanly for spontaneity. We know there was an outcome, because unanimity that size does not happen by accident. And we know it is connected to something larger, because three separate attendees, upon being asked for comment, began walking in the direction of the north pasture before remembering themselves and turning around.

The county says the gate investigation is ongoing. The council says the bench debate is unrelated. The herd says nothing, in forty-part harmony, every morning at eight.

Reporting live from a profession where that counts as progress. Back to you in the studio.
